What the Equilibrium Strategy is

A diver at neutral buoyancy neither sinks nor floats. Most of the day, a market behaves the same way. Buyers and sellers roughly agree on what something is worth, and price bobs up and down around that fair value. On a volume profile this shows up as a bulge, with the heaviest trading in the middle. We call that middle the equilibrium.

Every so often the ballast shifts. Price breaks out of the range, nobody pushes it back, and it drifts off to find a new fair value. That is imbalance.

The strategy is a set of rules for telling the two states apart in real time. In balance, you wait for price to stretch to the edge of value and get pushed back, then weave a trade toward equilibrium. In imbalance, you stop fading the move and either surface or follow it once it holds at new prices.

None of this predicts the tides. It gives you a reason for every trade, a place where you are clearly wrong, and a target that comes from the market rather than from hope.

    The wisdom strand

    Every basket needs two strands crossing each other. Ours are the chart and the trader. A strategy only works in the hands of someone who can follow it when it hurts, so half the course is about you. The ideas draw on Stoic philosophy, decision science and the plain experience of anyone who has broken their own rules.

    Each week pairs a market lesson with a practice: a journaling prompt, a pre-market routine, or a rule you commit to before the open.

    Patience

    Most of a good trader's day is waiting. You learn to treat no trade as a position you chose, like a diver holding still so the silt settles.

    Process over outcome

    A good trade can lose and a bad one can win. You grade yourself on whether you followed the plan.

    Mind your own depth

    Your mind has a safe range too. Tilt, revenge trading and boredom are what happen when you dive past it.

    Small enough to breathe

    Position size is a psychological tool. If a loss would change your mood, you're carrying too much weight.

    Questions

    If yours isn't here, write to hello@basketweaving.academy (sample address).

    Will I actually weave a basket?

    No. The baskets are spiritual. This is a day-trading course with a very committed sense of humor.

    Do I need to know how to swim?

    No experience needed. Week 1 covers charts, order types and how a trading day works. Experienced traders usually start at week 2.

    Which markets does this work in?

    Any liquid market with good volume data: large US stocks, index futures and major currency pairs. Course examples use stocks and index futures.

    Will I make money?

    We can't promise that and nobody honest can. The course teaches a method and the discipline to follow it. Most people who try day trading lose money, which is why every drill starts on a simulator.

    How much time does it take?

    Plan on three to four hours a week: about an hour of lessons and the rest on drills and journaling. No decompression stops required.

    Risk disclosure
